§ 07 · FAQ

The questions we get on every intro call.

If yours isn't here, email hello@whitedog.ops — we answer within a day.

Is this software we're licensing? +

No. We build a dashboard for your agency and hand it over — source code, database, the whole thing. You host it on your infrastructure (Vercel, Netlify, a VPS, whatever). We don't charge you monthly and we don't hold your data hostage.

What's actually in the $199 DIY kit? +

A complete Claude Code project with all the prompts, data schemas, and connector templates you need to build your own dashboard. Includes a working template to fork, step-by-step runbook, and access to a private Discord community of other agency operators building their own.

Will this work with our weird chart of accounts? +

Probably. We've seen a lot of charts of accounts. The kickoff session is specifically designed to map your setup to the dashboard. If your accounting is truly unusual, we'll know within the first 30 minutes and we'll tell you honestly if it's a fit.

What are the ongoing costs? +

None to us. You pay for your own hosting (usually free on Vercel/Netlify for a site this size) and any API costs from your existing tools. Most agencies spend $0-20/month on infrastructure. The 6 monthly check-ins are included in the $3,000.

What if a connector breaks? +

The runbook covers common failure modes. Most connector issues are resolved by re-authenticating or refreshing an API token. For trickier issues, the 6-month check-ins give you a direct line to us. After that, you can book ad-hoc support or maintain it in-house.

How long does it actually take? +

Three weeks from kickoff to handoff is typical. The actual build is usually done in the first 8 days. The remaining time is for iteration, tuning, and making sure your team is comfortable with the Monday-morning cadence.

Can we customize it after handoff? +

Yes. You own the source code. The two iteration rounds included in the build are for us to tune it with you, but after handoff you're free to modify, extend, or fork it. Many agencies have their own dev teams make ongoing tweaks.
